## 1. Journal Submission Abstract (Structured, 248 Words — Springer JIIM / MBEC Compliant)

- **Purpose:** Deep convolutional neural networks achieve high diagnostic accuracy in brain tumour MRI classification, but may exploit non-tumour context shortcuts. While spatial tile scrambling severely degrades model performance, it inadvertently injects synthetic high-frequency boundary seams. We investigate whether this performance collapse reflects sensitivity to macroscopic cranial configuration, synthetic boundary-step artifacts, or compound out-of-distribution disorientation.
- **Methods:** On the 3,064-slice Cheng Figshare dataset (233 patient clusters; meningioma, glioma, pituitary tumour), 15 frozen ResNet-50 checkpoints were evaluated across three patient-aware cross-validation scenarios ($S_0, S_1, S_2$) under six standardized conditions: original baseline ($C_{0\text{,orig}}$), pure 2-pixel boundary seam control ($C_{1\text{,true\_seam\_w2}}$), amplified boundary seam control (gain 1.8; legacy identifier w3, not a three-pixel width) ($C_{1\text{,true\_seam\_w3}}$), historical whole-tile DC-offset control ($C_{1\text{,historical\_dc\_offset}}$), locally blended spatial permutation ($C_{2\text{,seam\_suppressed}}$), and raw tile scrambling ($C_{3\text{,c5lp\_scramble}}$). Tumour core and 15-pixel margin rings were 100% protected (zero altered pixels). Across 55,152 forward predictions, uncertainty was quantified using synchronized paired patient-cluster bootstrapping ($B=25,000$) with family-wise Holm-Bonferroni FWER control and exact binomial McNemar tests.
- **Results:** In patient-disjoint split $S_0$, raw tile scrambling degraded Macro-F1 from 0.9333 to 0.5118 ($\Delta = +0.4215$, exact McNemar $p < 10^{-15}$). Pure 2-pixel boundary steps alone induced a modest drop to 0.8127 ($\Delta = +0.1206$), while historical DC offsets reduced Macro-F1 to 0.6290 ($\Delta = +0.3043$). Crucially, seam-suppressed tile permutation ($C_2$) still caused a severe collapse to 0.4525 ($\Delta = +0.4808$, 95% CI: $[0.4360, 0.5280]$)—fourfold larger than pure boundary seams alone. Spatial permutation induced a monotonic dose-dependent flip of true supratentorial gliomas to skull-base pituitary tumour (0.14% baseline $\to$ 16.48% in $C_{1\text{,w2}} \to$ 23.35% in $C_{1\text{,w3}} \to$ 31.28% in $C_{1\text{,hist}} \to$ 56.45% in $C_3 \to$ 63.53% in $C_2$).
- **Conclusion:** Performance collapse is governed by a compound, multi-factor mechanism. While CNNs exhibit measurable sensitivity to high-frequency edge steps alone, spatial permutation introduces severe contextual disorientation. However, scrambling conflates macroscopic layout with non-local tissue juxtapositions, residual boundary steps, and out-of-distribution inputs; therefore, causal attribution to independent continuous anatomical coordinates cannot be established. Spatial perturbation studies must explicitly account for multi-property confounding.

**Keywords:** Brain tumour classification; Convolutional neural networks; Magnetic resonance imaging; Shortcut learning; Spatial context; Image perturbation; Artifact disentanglement; Reproducibility.

## 2. Extended Comprehensive Abstract (Archival / Preprint)

### Background
Deep neural networks achieve high diagnostic accuracy in brain tumour MRI classification, yet emerging evidence warns that nominal performance can be inflated by shortcut learning on non-tumour image features. Spatial perturbation studies, such as tile scrambling, have reported severe performance collapses when distant cranial contexts are rearranged, suggesting heavy model reliance on macroscopic anatomical layout. However, spatial tile scrambling simultaneously introduces prominent synthetic high-frequency step discontinuities along tile seams, creating a critical methodological confound: does model failure reflect true reliance on cranial spatial organization, or is it an artifactual vulnerability to synthetic edge discontinuities?

### Methods
We conducted a controlled multi-intervention study governed by a pre-specified analytical protocol on the Cheng Figshare brain tumour MRI cohort (3,064 2D T1-contrast-enhanced slices across 233 patient clusters, encompassing meningioma, glioma, and pituitary tumour). Using 15 frozen ResNet-50 checkpoints evaluated across three cross-validation scenarios ($S_0$: patient-disjoint, $S_1$: near-duplicate cleaned at SSIM 0.80, and $S_2$: aggressive grouping at SSIM 0.75), we operationalized six standardized matched conditions: (1) original baseline ($C_{0\text{,orig}}$); (2) pure 2-pixel boundary step seam control ($C_{1\text{,true\_seam\_w2}}$); (3) amplified boundary step seam control (gain 1.8; legacy identifier w3) ($C_{1\text{,true\_seam\_w3}}$); (4) historical whole-tile DC-offset control ($C_{1\text{,historical\_dc\_offset}}$); (5) locally blended spatial permutation ($C_{2\text{,seam\_suppressed}}$), permuting distant tiles while applying a distance-weighted Gaussian blend; quantitative seam attenuation remains pending because the earlier diagnostic used a different operator; and (6) raw tile scrambling ($C_{3\text{,c5lp\_scramble}}$). In all conditions, the tumour core and a 15-pixel dilation margin ring were 100% protected (zero pixels altered). Across 55,152 matched out-of-fold forward evaluations, uncertainty was quantified using synchronized paired cluster bootstrapping ($B=25,000$ resamples) with family-wise Holm-Bonferroni FWER multiplicity control and exact binomial McNemar calculations.

### Results
In the patient-disjoint split ($S_0$), the raw scrambling drop replicated with high fidelity ($\Delta \text{Macro-F1} = +0.4215$, 95% CI: $[0.3722, 0.4713]$, exact McNemar $p < 10^{-15}$). Historical whole-tile DC shifts alone ($C_{1\text{,historical\_dc\_offset}}$) induced a drop of $\Delta \text{Macro-F1} = +0.3043$ (95% CI: $[0.2581, 0.3530]$, $p < 10^{-15}$), while pure 2-pixel boundary steps dropped Macro-F1 to 0.8127 ($\Delta = +0.1206$, 95% CI: $[0.0888, 0.1565]$), representing an empirical ratio of 28.6% of the raw scrambling drop. Crucially, seam-suppressed tile permutation ($C_{2\text{,seam\_suppressed}}$) still caused a severe performance collapse of $\Delta \text{Macro-F1} = +0.4808$ (95% CI: $[0.4360, 0.5280]$, $p < 10^{-15}$), larger than the gain-1.0 seam-only comparison in this particular experiment and substantially exceeding our pre-defined significance threshold of $0.150$. Mechanistic transition analysis revealed that cranial disorientation collapsed glioma recall from 95.0% to 8.6%, with 63.5% of gliomas misclassified into the skull-base pituitary class following a monotonic disorientation dose-response gradient (0.14% in $C_0$, 16.48% in $C_{1\text{,w2}}$, 23.35% in $C_{1\text{,w3}}$, 31.28% in $C_{1\text{,hist}}$, 56.45% in $C_3$, and 63.53% in $C_2$).

### Conclusions
Our findings demonstrate that performance collapse under tile scrambling is driven by a compound mechanism: while artificial boundary discontinuities alone degrade CNN feature extraction, spatial rearrangement causes severe diagnostic failure. Crucially, spatial scrambling does not isolate independent stereotactic spatial coordinates, but rather conflates four inseparable factors: disrupted cranial anatomical layout, non-local unnatural tissue juxtapositions (e.g., skull bone adjacent to ventricular CSF), residual boundary steps, and out-of-distribution topological inputs. Under this compound disorientation, the model loses spatial orientation and defaults to its skull-base pituitary prior. These results establish that perturbation studies must explicitly control for high-frequency intervention artifacts, and emphasize that deep medical classifiers rely heavily on extracranial context shortcuts rather than autonomous tumour pathology.
